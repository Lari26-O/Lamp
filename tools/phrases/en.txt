Baby Jesus is born
In the little town of Nazareth lived a young woman named Mary. She loved God, and she was going to marry a carpenter named Joseph. Nazareth was a small, quiet place with dusty roads and little houses made of stone. Mary helped her family, fetched water from the well, and prayed to God every day.
One day the angel Gabriel appeared to Mary. Don’t be afraid, Mary! God is happy with you. You will have a baby boy, and you will name him Jesus. He is the Son of God! Mary was very surprised. How can this be? she asked. The angel said: The Holy Spirit will come upon you. Nothing is impossible for God!
Mary said: I am God’s servant. Let it happen just as you said. An angel also spoke to Joseph in a dream, so Joseph took Mary home to be his wife. The angel told Joseph: Do not be afraid to take Mary as your wife. The baby is from God. He will save his people from their sins. When Joseph woke up, he did just what the angel said.
The emperor wanted to count all the people, so everyone had to go to their family’s town. Mary and Joseph traveled a long way to Bethlehem, the town of King David. The trip took many days. They walked up and down rocky hills. At night they slept under the stars. Mary was tired, because the baby would be born very soon.
When they got to Bethlehem, the town was full of people. There was no room for them in the guest room. Where would the baby be born? Joseph knocked on door after door. Sorry, no room here! Sorry, we are full! Finally they found a quiet place where the animals were kept.
That night, baby Jesus was born! Mary wrapped him snugly in cloths and laid him in a manger, the box where animals eat their hay. The cows and donkeys watched quietly. Mary and Joseph looked at the tiny baby with so much love. God’s own Son had come to earth, not in a palace, but in a simple stable.
Nearby, shepherds were watching their sheep in the dark fields. Suddenly an angel appeared, shining bright! Good news of great joy! Today a Savior is born for you! Then many angels sang: Glory to God! The shepherds were shaking with fear. But the angel said: Don’t be afraid! You will find the baby wrapped in cloths and lying in a manger. That is how you will know him.
The shepherds hurried to Bethlehem and found baby Jesus lying in the manger, just as the angel said. They told everyone what they had heard, and went back praising God! Everyone who heard was amazed. Mary kept all these things in her heart and thought about them often. God had kept his promise to send a Savior for the whole world!
God sent his Son Jesus as a little baby. He was born in Bethlehem and laid in a manger. Angels told the shepherds the good news: a Savior is born!
Jesus is God’s Son. He came because God loves us.
A Savior has been born to you; he is Christ the Lord.

Jesus picks his friends
Jesus grew up and began to teach people about God. One morning he was standing by the Sea of Galilee, and a big crowd pushed close to hear him. The water sparkled in the morning sun. Everyone wanted to be close to Jesus, because his words were full of love and truth about God.
Two fishing boats were on the shore. The fishermen were washing their nets. They had worked hard all night long. Their nets were empty, and they were tired and a little sad. They had thrown the nets again and again in the dark, but not one fish had come up.
Jesus got into Simon Peter’s boat and asked him to push out a little from the shore. Then Jesus sat down and taught the people from the boat. From the boat, everyone on the beach could see and hear Jesus. Peter sat close by and listened to every word.
When he finished, Jesus said: Go out into deep water and let down your nets to catch fish. Peter was a fisherman, and he knew the best time to fish was at night, not in the bright daytime. What would he do?
Peter said: Master, we worked all night and caught nothing. But because you say so, I will let down the nets. So Peter rowed out to the deep water and threw the heavy net over the side. Splash! It sank down, down, down.
Splash! The nets filled up with so many fish that they began to tear! Peter called his partners James and John to come help. Both boats were so full they almost sank! The fish wiggled and jumped and splashed! Everyone pulled as hard as they could. They had never seen so many fish in their whole lives!
Peter fell down at Jesus’ knees. He knew Jesus was special. Jesus said kindly: Don’t be afraid. From now on you will fish for people! Peter said: Go away from me, Lord, I am a sinful man! But Jesus did not go away. He wanted Peter to be his friend and to help bring people to God.
They pulled their boats up on the shore, left everything behind, and followed Jesus. Peter, Andrew, James and John became Jesus’ first friends and helpers. They left their nets, their boats and all those fish! Being with Jesus was more important than anything else. From that day on, they went everywhere with him.
Peter caught nothing all night. When he obeyed Jesus, the nets filled with fish! Then Peter and his friends left everything to follow Jesus.
Jesus wants me to be his friend.
Jesus said: Come, follow me.

Jesus stops the storm
Jesus had been teaching by the lake all day long. When evening came, he said to his friends: Let’s go over to the other side. The sun was setting and the sky turned orange and pink. The water was calm and smooth like glass.
They left the crowd and got into the boat. Other little boats came with them across the water. The friends raised the sail and the wind pushed the boat gently across the lake. Several of them were fishermen, so they knew all about boats.
Jesus was very tired. He went to the back of the boat, laid his head on a cushion, and fell fast asleep. The boat rocked softly back and forth. Jesus slept peacefully, like a baby in a cradle.
Suddenly a furious storm came! Whoosh went the wind! The waves crashed over the sides, and the boat began to fill with water. The sky turned dark. Rain poured down and lightning flashed. Even the fishermen had never seen a storm this big!
The friends were terrified. They shook Jesus awake: Teacher, don’t you care that we are going to drown? They bailed water out of the boat as fast as they could. But the waves kept coming. And Jesus was still sleeping!
Jesus got up. He spoke to the wind, and he said to the waves: Quiet! Be still! Jesus was not afraid at all. He stood up in the rocking boat, with the wind blowing his hair and the rain on his face.
Right away the wind stopped, and the lake became completely calm. Not one little wave! Jesus asked: Why are you so afraid? Do you still not trust me? The clouds rolled away and the stars came out. Everything was quiet. The only sound was the water softly touching the boat.
The friends looked at each other in wonder. Who is this? Even the wind and the waves obey him! They were learning that Jesus is not just a teacher. He is God’s Son, and he has power over everything, even storms. When we are afraid, we can call to Jesus too.
A big storm came while Jesus slept in the boat. His friends were afraid. Jesus said: Be still! and the storm stopped. Jesus is stronger than any storm.
Jesus can do anything. I don’t have to be afraid.
When I am afraid, I will trust in you.
What makes you scared? Jesus is with you.

A boy shares his lunch
A huge crowd followed Jesus up a hillside, because they saw how he healed sick people. There were about five thousand men, plus women and children! People came from every town and village. Moms carried babies, dads held their children’s hands, and grandparents walked slowly behind.
Jesus sat down with his friends and taught the people. The hours went by, and the sun began to go down. Jesus healed the sick and talked about God’s love. Nobody wanted to leave, even though they had walked a long way and had not eaten.
Jesus asked Philip: Where can we buy bread for all these people to eat? Philip said: Even lots of money would not buy enough for everyone to have a bite! Jesus already knew what he was going to do. He asked Philip to see if he would trust him.
Andrew said: Here is a boy with five small barley loaves and two little fish. But what is that for so many people? Barley loaves were the bread that poor people ate. It was just enough lunch for one hungry boy. But the boy was happy to share it with Jesus.
The boy gave his lunch to Jesus. Jesus said: Have the people sit down. So everyone sat down on the soft green grass. The people sat in big groups, like colorful patches on a green blanket. Everyone wondered what Jesus was going to do.
Jesus took the loaves, looked up and thanked God for the food. Then he gave out the bread and the fish, and it did not run out! His friends carried the food to the people, group after group. Every time they came back, there was still more bread and more fish to give!
Everyone ate as much as they wanted, until they were all full. Thousands of people, fed with one boy’s small lunch! The children laughed with full tummies. The grown-ups shook their heads in wonder. Only God could do something like this!
Jesus said: Gather the pieces that are left so nothing is wasted. His friends filled twelve baskets with leftovers! The people said: Surely this is the one God promised! The boy had given a little, and Jesus turned it into a lot. When we give what we have to Jesus, he can do amazing things with it.
A boy gave Jesus his five loaves and two fish. Jesus thanked God and fed more than five thousand people, with twelve baskets left over! A little given to Jesus becomes a lot.
When I share, Jesus can do great things.
Do good and share with others.

Jesus loves children
Everywhere Jesus went, people came to see him. Some came to learn, and some came to be healed. There were always so many people around Jesus! Sick people, sad people, curious people. His friends tried to help keep things in order.
One day, moms and dads brought their little children to Jesus. They wanted Jesus to put his hands on them and pray for them. The parents had heard how kind Jesus was. If Jesus would just touch their children and bless them, they knew it would be wonderful.
The children were so excited to meet Jesus! Some were carried, and some ran ahead down the path. Some of the children had never seen Jesus before. Is it true he loves children? they asked. Let’s go and see!
But Jesus’ disciples stopped them. They scolded the parents: Don’t bother Jesus! He is too busy for children. The disciples thought Jesus had more important things to do. They thought children were too small and too noisy to matter.
The children stopped and looked down sadly. Maybe Jesus did not want to see them after all. A little girl held her mommy’s hand tightly. A little boy wiped a tear from his cheek. They turned to go home.
When Jesus saw this, he was not happy with his disciples. He said: Let the little children come to me! Don’t stop them! Everyone turned to look. Jesus was smiling at the children with open arms. The children’s faces lit up!
God’s kingdom belongs to people who are like these children. Anyone who wants to be part of it must trust God just like a little child. Children trust with their whole heart. Jesus wants all of us to trust God like that, and to come to him just as we are.
Then Jesus took the children in his arms. He put his hands on them, one by one, and blessed them. Jesus always has time for children! The children laughed and hugged Jesus back. They went home knowing something very special: Jesus loves me!
Parents brought their children to Jesus, but the disciples tried to send them away. Jesus said: Let the children come to me! He hugged them and blessed them. Jesus loves you!
Jesus always has time for me.
Let the little children come to me.

The kind helper
A teacher of God’s law asked Jesus: What must I do to live forever with God? Jesus said: What does God’s law say? The man knew the Bible very well. He wanted to see if Jesus would give a good answer.
The man said: Love God with all your heart, and love your neighbor as yourself. Then he asked: But who is my neighbor? So Jesus told a story. Jesus said: That’s right! Do this and you will live. But the man wanted to know exactly who he had to love, and who he did not.
A man was walking down the rocky road from Jerusalem to Jericho. Robbers jumped out, hurt him, took his clothes and money, and left him lying by the road. The road was long and lonely, winding through the rocky hills. The poor man lay there, hurt and alone, hoping someone would help him.
A priest came down the same road. When he saw the hurt man, he crossed to the other side and kept walking. The priest served in God’s temple. He should have helped! But maybe he was in a hurry, or afraid. He did not even stop to look.
Then a Levite, a helper in God’s temple, came by. He looked at the man, but he also passed by on the other side. The hurt man was still lying there. The sun was hot, and he was getting weaker. Would anyone help him?
Then a man from Samaria came along. Samaritans and Jews were not friends. But when he saw the hurt man, his heart was full of kindness. Nobody expected a Samaritan to help a Jewish man. But he did not care where the man was from. He only saw someone who needed help.
He knelt down, cleaned the man’s cuts with oil and wine, and wrapped them in bandages. Then he put the man on his own donkey and took him to an inn. He walked beside the donkey all the way, making sure the man did not fall. At the inn he stayed with him all night and took care of him.
He paid the innkeeper and said: Take care of him. I will pay for anything else when I come back. Jesus asked: Which one was a neighbor? The one who was kind. Jesus said: Go and do the same. A neighbor is not just someone who lives next door. A neighbor is anyone who needs our help, even someone very different from us.
A man was hurt on the road. Two people walked past, but a Samaritan stopped, cared for him and paid for his rest. Jesus said: Love everyone, like the kind Samaritan.
Jesus wants me to help everyone, not just my friends.

Jesus helps a man see
Jesus and his friends were walking through the city of Jericho, and a big crowd was following them. Jericho was a big city with tall palm trees. People crowded the streets, hoping to see Jesus.
By the side of the road sat a man named Bartimaeus. He was blind, so every day he sat there and begged for help. He could not see the blue sky or the faces of the people walking by. He could only hear them, and sometimes someone dropped a coin in his cup.
Bartimaeus heard lots of feet and voices. Someone told him: Jesus of Nazareth is coming by! Bartimaeus had heard stories about Jesus. Jesus made sick people well and helped blind people see! This was his chance.
Bartimaeus shouted: Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me! Son of David was a special name for the King God had promised. Bartimaeus believed that Jesus was that King.
Many people told him: Be quiet! But he shouted even louder: Son of David, have mercy on me! He did not give up! He knew Jesus could help him, and he wanted Jesus to hear him.
Jesus stopped and said: Call him. The people said to Bartimaeus: Cheer up! Get up! He is calling you! He threw off his coat, jumped up and came to Jesus. His coat was probably the most important thing he owned. But he left it behind, because Jesus was calling him!
Jesus asked him: What do you want me to do for you? Bartimaeus said: Teacher, I want to see! Jesus already knew he was blind. But Jesus wanted to hear Bartimaeus ask. Jesus loves when we tell him what we need.
Jesus said: Go, your faith has healed you. Right away Bartimaeus could see! He saw colors, faces, and Jesus smiling at him. And he followed Jesus down the road. The first face Bartimaeus ever saw was the face of Jesus! He praised God, and everyone who saw it praised God too.
Blind Bartimaeus kept calling to Jesus, even when people told him to stop. Jesus asked what he wanted and healed his eyes. Then Bartimaeus followed Jesus.
Jesus hears me when I call him.
I called to the Lord, and he heard me.
You can talk to Jesus anytime. What would you ask him?

Zacchaeus climbs a tree
In the city of Jericho lived a man named Zacchaeus. He was the chief tax collector, and he was very rich. He had a big house, fine clothes and lots of gold coins. But he did not have many friends.
Zacchaeus often took more money than he should, and kept it for himself. That is why people did not like him. People whispered when he walked by. Deep inside, Zacchaeus was lonely, and his heart was not happy.
One day, Jesus came to Jericho! Zacchaeus wanted to see who Jesus was. But he was a short man, and the crowd was too thick. He jumped up and down and stood on his tiptoes, but all he could see were backs and heads!
So Zacchaeus ran ahead and climbed up a sycamore-fig tree, right where Jesus was going to pass. He climbed higher and higher and sat on a strong branch. From up there, he could see the whole road. Here comes Jesus!
When Jesus reached the spot, he stopped, looked up and said: Zacchaeus, come down quickly! I must stay at your house today. Zacchaeus almost fell out of the tree! Jesus knew his name! And Jesus wanted to come to his house!
Zacchaeus climbed down as fast as he could and welcomed Jesus with joy. But the people grumbled: Jesus is going to the house of a sinner! The people did not understand why Jesus would visit someone who had cheated them. But Jesus loved Zacchaeus, even before he changed.
Zacchaeus stood up and said: Lord, I will give half of everything I own to the poor. And if I cheated anyone, I will pay them back four times as much! Being with Jesus changed him! Now he wanted to make things right and be generous instead of greedy.
Jesus said: Today salvation has come to this house! I came to look for the lost and to save them. Jesus changed Zacchaeus’s heart. Zacchaeus was not lonely anymore. He had found the best friend of all, and his heart was full of joy.
Zacchaeus was short and had cheated people. He climbed a tree to see Jesus, and Jesus came to his house! Zacchaeus said sorry and gave back what he took. Jesus changes hearts.
Jesus loves me even when I do wrong. I can say sorry.
Jesus came to find and save the lost.

The lost sheep
Many people came to listen to Jesus. Some leaders grumbled: Jesus welcomes sinners and even eats with them! So Jesus told them a story. The leaders thought that God only loved good people. Jesus wanted them to understand how much God loves everyone who is lost.
Once there was a shepherd who had one hundred sheep. He knew every one of them, and he took good care of them. He even knew which sheep liked to run ahead, and which ones liked to stay close to him.
Every day he led them to green grass and fresh water. At night he counted them to make sure they were all safe. With his staff he guided them and kept wolves away. The sheep trusted their shepherd and followed his voice.
One evening he counted: ninety-seven, ninety-eight, ninety-nine… Oh no! One little sheep was missing! He counted again, very carefully. Still ninety-nine. The smallest lamb was gone!
Far away, the little sheep was lost among the rocks and thorny bushes. It was cold and scared. Baa! Baa! The lamb had wandered off to eat some grass, and then it could not find its way back. It was getting dark.
The shepherd left the ninety-nine sheep safe together, and went out to look for the lost one. He searched and searched until he found it. He climbed over rocks and pushed through prickly bushes. He did not stop, even when he was tired. Then he heard a tiny baa!
He lifted the little sheep onto his shoulders, so happy! And he carried it all the way home. He was not angry with the little sheep. He was just so glad to have it back! The lamb snuggled close, safe at last.
He called his friends and neighbors: Celebrate with me! I found my lost sheep! Jesus said: In the same way, there is great joy in heaven when one person comes back to God. Jesus is our Good Shepherd. When we wander away from God, he comes looking for us, and he is so happy when we come home.
A shepherd had one hundred sheep, and one got lost. He searched until he found it, carried it home and had a party. Jesus loves each one of us that much!
Jesus is my good shepherd. He never stops looking for me.
Jesus said: I am the good shepherd.
How does it feel to know Jesus will always find you?

Love one another
It was the night before Jesus would die. Jesus knew it was almost time to go back to his Father, and he loved his friends very much. It was the time of the Passover feast, when God’s people remembered how God had saved them from Egypt long ago.
Jesus and his twelve friends were eating a special dinner together in an upstairs room. There was bread, and cups to drink from. The friends talked and ate together, but they did not know this was their last dinner with Jesus before he died.
In those days people walked everywhere in sandals, so their feet got very dusty. Usually a servant washed the guests’ feet. But there was no servant that night. Washing feet was a job for the lowest servant. None of the friends wanted to do it. Some of them were even arguing about who was the greatest!
Jesus got up from the table. He took off his outer robe, wrapped a towel around his waist, and poured water into a bowl. Everyone grew quiet. What was Jesus doing? He was the Teacher! He was God’s Son!
Then Jesus knelt down and began to wash his friends’ feet, one by one, and dry them with the towel. The water was cool and the towel was soft. Jesus washed each dusty foot gently, even the feet of Judas, who would turn against him.
Peter said: Lord, you will never wash my feet! Jesus said: If I don’t wash you, you can’t be with me. Peter said: Then wash my hands and my head too! Peter loved Jesus very much. He did not want Jesus to serve him. But Jesus was teaching him something important about love.
When he finished, Jesus sat down again. He said: I am your Lord and Teacher, and I washed your feet. Now you should serve each other the same way. Being great is not about being the boss. It is about helping others, even with jobs nobody else wants to do.
Then Jesus gave them a new command: Love one another. Just as I have loved you, you must love one another. That is how everyone will know you are my friends. Jesus showed his love by serving, and soon he would show the greatest love of all by giving his life for us.
At his last supper, Jesus knelt down and washed his friends’ dusty feet, like a servant. Then he said: Love one another, just as I have loved you.
I can show love by helping others.

Jesus is alive!
Jesus rode into Jerusalem on a young donkey. People waved palm branches and shouted: Hosanna! But some leaders were jealous and wanted to get rid of him. Jesus had taught them, healed the sick and loved everyone. Many people loved him too. But the jealous leaders started to make a plan.
That week, soldiers arrested Jesus in a garden at night. Jesus had done nothing wrong, but they decided he must die. Jesus had been praying in the garden. He knew what was going to happen, but he chose to obey his Father, because of his great love for us.
Jesus died on a cross. He did it because he loves us. He took the punishment for all the wrong things we do, so we can be forgiven and be friends with God. The sky became dark in the middle of the day. Jesus prayed: Father, forgive them. Then he said: It is finished. He had done what he came to do.
His friends were very, very sad. They laid his body in a tomb cut into the rock, and a big round stone was rolled in front of the door. Soldiers guarded it. It was very quiet. Jesus’ friends stayed home, sad and afraid. They had forgotten that Jesus had said he would rise again on the third day.
Early on Sunday morning, Mary Magdalene and other women came to the tomb with sweet-smelling spices. They wondered: Who will roll the big stone away for us? The sun was just coming up. Birds were singing. The women walked slowly, with sad hearts.
But when they got there, the stone was already rolled away! An angel in shining white sat on it. The tomb was empty! There had been an earthquake, and the soldiers had fallen down like dead men. The women looked inside: only the folded cloths were there.
The angel said: Don’t be afraid! You are looking for Jesus. He is not here. He is risen, just as he said! Go and tell his friends! Remember? He told you he would rise again! The women’s hearts began to beat fast with joy.
The women ran with joy! Then Jesus himself met them and said: Greetings! Later he visited all his friends. They touched him and ate with him. Jesus is alive! Death could not keep Jesus in the tomb. Because Jesus is alive, everyone who believes in him will live with him forever!
Jesus died on the cross to take away our sins because he loves us. He was put in a tomb, but on Sunday the tomb was empty. Jesus came back to life! He is alive forever.
Jesus died for me and rose again. He is alive!
He is not here; he has risen!

Jesus is always with me
After Jesus rose from the dead, he showed himself to his friends many times for forty days. He talked with them about God’s kingdom. He ate with them, talked with them, and let them touch the marks in his hands. Now they knew for sure: Jesus is really alive!
One day his eleven friends met him on a mountain in Galilee. When they saw Jesus, they worshiped him. The wind blew gently on the mountain top. The friends knelt down. Their teacher was also their Lord and their God.
Jesus said: Go to all the nations and make disciples. Baptize them, and teach them to obey everything I have taught you. Jesus wanted every person in every country to hear that God loves them. His friends had an important job to do!
And remember: I am with you always, to the very end of the world! Wherever they went, they would never be alone. Jesus would always be with them, and he is always with us too.
Later, near Jerusalem, Jesus said: Wait here. The Holy Spirit will come and give you power to tell everyone about me. You will tell people about me in Jerusalem, in all the land around it, and to the ends of the earth.
Then, while they watched, Jesus was taken up into the sky, and a cloud hid him from their sight. Jesus lifted up his hands and blessed them. Then he went up, up, up, until they could not see him anymore.
As they kept looking up, two men in white stood beside them. Why are you looking at the sky? Jesus will come back the same way you saw him go! The friends were filled with great joy. They went back to Jerusalem praising God and waiting for the Holy Spirit.
Soon the Holy Spirit came, just as Jesus promised. His friends went everywhere telling the good news. And today Jesus is still with everyone who loves him, even you! Thousands of people believed in Jesus. The good news spread from town to town and country to country, until it reached you!
Before going back to heaven, Jesus told his friends: Go and tell everyone about me. He promised to send the Holy Spirit and said: I am with you always. Jesus is with you too!
Jesus is with me everywhere I go.
I am with you always.

God made everything
In the beginning, there was nothing at all: no sky, no ground, no people. Only God. The world was dark and empty. But God was there, and God had a wonderful plan. God’s Spirit moved over the dark waters.
On the first day, God said: Let there be light! And there was light. God called the light day, and the darkness night. God only had to speak, and it happened! Light filled everything, and God saw that the light was good.
On the second day, God made the big blue sky. On the third day, God gathered the waters into seas and made dry land appear. Now there were mountains and valleys, beaches and rivers. The sea waves splashed on the sand.
On the land God made grass, flowers, and trees full of fruit. On the fourth day he made the sun for the day, and the moon and stars for the night. Apples, figs, grapes and oranges grew on the trees. The sun warmed the earth, and the stars twinkled in the night sky.
On the fifth day, God filled the seas with fish and whales, and the sky with birds. Splash! Tweet tweet! Tiny fish and giant whales, colorful parrots and big eagles. God blessed them and said: Have lots of babies and fill the earth!
On the sixth day, God made all the animals: elephants and giraffes, lions and lambs, bunnies and tiny bugs! Some animals were fast and some were slow. Some were furry, some had feathers, and some had shells. Every one was special.
Then God made people, a man and a woman, Adam and Eve. He made them to be like him, to love him and take care of his world. God made you too! God formed Adam from the dust of the ground and breathed life into him. Then God made Eve. God put them in a beautiful garden called Eden.
God looked at everything he had made, and it was very good! On the seventh day, God rested. God made a special day to rest and enjoy everything he had made. He wants us to rest and remember him too.
In six days God made light, the sky, the land and seas, plants, the sun, moon and stars, fish and birds, animals, and people. Everything was very good, and on the seventh day God rested.

Noah and the big boat
Long after creation, the world was full of people who did bad things and forgot God. This made God very sad. People were unkind and hurt each other. They did not listen to God at all.
But one man, Noah, loved God and obeyed him. God said: Noah, a big flood is coming. Build an ark, a huge wooden boat, for you and your family. God gave Noah exact instructions: how long, how wide, and how tall to make the ark, with a roof, a door and three floors inside.
Noah did everything just as God told him. Bang, bang, bang! He built and built, even though people laughed. The ark was taller than a house and as long as a football field! It took a very long time. Noah’s sons Shem, Ham and Japheth helped him cut the wood and cover it with tar to keep the water out.
Then God brought the animals to the ark, two by two, a boy and a girl of every kind: lions, giraffes, elephants, sheep, and even little bunnies! Noah also packed lots and lots of food for his family and for all the animals.
Noah, his wife, their three sons and their wives went inside, and God shut the door. Then the rain came down: drip, drop, splash! It rained for forty days and forty nights. Water came down from the sky and up from the ground. The ark began to float higher and higher.
The water covered even the highest mountains. But inside the ark, Noah, his family and all the animals were safe and dry. God remembered them. Inside, the animals ate and slept. Noah and his family took care of them every day, and they waited for God to dry the land.
After many months the water went down and the ark rested on a mountain. Noah sent out a dove. It came back with a fresh olive leaf! The land was drying. A week later Noah sent the dove again, and this time it did not come back. It had found a home! God told Noah: Come out of the ark.
Everyone came out of the ark, and Noah thanked God. God put a beautiful rainbow in the clouds and said: This is my promise. I will never again flood the whole earth. The animals ran and hopped and flew out into the fresh new world. Every time we see a rainbow, we can remember that God keeps his promises.
Noah obeyed God and built a big ark. God kept Noah, his family and the animals safe in the flood. Then God gave the rainbow as a promise. God always keeps his promises!
God always keeps his promises.

Abraham counts the stars
Abram and his wife Sarai lived in a big city called Haran. One day God spoke to Abram: Leave your home and go to a land I will show you. It was not easy to leave his home, his friends and everything he knew. But God had something better for him.
God promised: I will bless you and make you into a great nation. Through your family, all the people on earth will be blessed! That was a very big promise! Jesus would one day be born into Abraham’s family, and bring God’s blessing to the whole world.
Abram did not know where he was going, but he trusted God. He packed up his tents, his sheep and camels, and set off with Sarai and his nephew Lot. Abram was seventy-five years old. The camels swayed as they walked, and the sheep followed behind. Where are we going? Wherever God leads us!
They traveled a long way to the land of Canaan. There Abram built an altar to worship God and thank him. God said: I will give this land to your children. Abram did not have any children yet, but he believed God anyway.
Years went by. Abram and Sarai grew very old, and they still had no children. Abram asked God: How can my family become great if I have no child? Sarai’s hair turned gray, and Abram walked with a cane. They wondered if God had forgotten his promise. But God never forgets.
One night God took Abram outside. Look up at the sky and count the stars, if you can! That is how many people will be in your family. Abram looked up at the sky. There were so many stars, more than he could ever count!
Abram believed God, and God was pleased with him. God gave them new names: Abraham, which means father of many, and Sarah. Believing God means trusting that what he says is true, even before we see it happen.
When Abraham was one hundred years old, God kept his promise. Sarah had a baby boy! They named him Isaac, which means laughter, because they were so happy. Sarah said: God has brought me laughter! Everyone who hears about this will laugh with me. Nothing is too hard for God!
God told Abraham to go to a new land, and Abraham trusted God. God promised him a family as many as the stars. Even though they were very old, God gave Abraham and Sarah a son, Isaac.
I can trust God, even when I don’t know the way.

Joseph forgives his brothers
Jacob had twelve sons, and Joseph was his favorite. Jacob gave Joseph a beautiful robe with many colors. His brothers saw it and became very jealous. The brothers would not say one kind word to Joseph. Every time they saw his coat, they felt angry inside.
Joseph had dreams that one day his family would bow down to him. When he told his brothers, they were even angrier. In one dream, the brothers’ bundles of wheat bowed down to Joseph’s bundle. In another, the sun, the moon and eleven stars bowed to him!
One day the brothers grabbed Joseph, took his colorful robe, and sold him to traders going to Egypt. They told their father that a wild animal had killed him. Old Jacob cried and cried. Joseph was taken far away to a land where he did not know anyone or even speak the language.
In Egypt, Joseph worked as a servant, and later he was even put in prison for something he did not do. But God was with Joseph the whole time. Even in prison, Joseph worked hard and was kind to others. God helped him, and the jailer put him in charge of the other prisoners.
One night Pharaoh, the king of Egypt, had strange dreams. God helped Joseph explain them: Seven years of good harvests are coming, and then seven years of hunger. In the dreams, seven fat cows were eaten by seven skinny cows. Joseph said: I cannot explain dreams, but God can.
Pharaoh put Joseph in charge of all Egypt! Joseph stored up lots and lots of grain. When the hungry years came, people from everywhere came to Egypt for food. Joseph wore fine clothes and a gold chain, and rode in Pharaoh’s chariot. Only Pharaoh was more important than him!
Joseph’s brothers came too, and they bowed down to him. They did not know it was Joseph! Joseph tested them, and saw that they were sorry for what they had done. Just like his dream long ago, his brothers were bowing down to him! Joseph had to turn away so they would not see him cry.
Joseph cried and said: I am Joseph, your brother! You meant to hurt me, but God used it for good, to save many lives. I forgive you. Then the whole family, even old Jacob, came to live with Joseph. Joseph could have been angry or gotten even. Instead, he chose to forgive. He hugged his old father and cried with joy.
Joseph’s jealous brothers sold him to Egypt. God was with Joseph and made him a great leader who saved people from hunger. When his brothers came, Joseph forgave them.

Baby Moses in a basket
Many years later, Joseph’s family had grown into a big nation in Egypt, called the Israelites. A new Pharaoh was afraid of them, so he made them work very hard as slaves. They made bricks from mud and straw under the hot sun all day long. They cried out to God for help.
Then Pharaoh made a terrible rule: every baby boy born to the Israelites must be thrown into the Nile River. The families were so sad and scared. But some brave people did not obey the cruel king, because they loved God more.
An Israelite mother had a beautiful baby boy. She loved him so much that she hid him at home for three months. She sang soft songs to keep him quiet, and her heart jumped every time someone knocked at the door.
When she could not hide him any longer, she made a little basket out of reeds, covered it with tar so it would float, and put her baby inside. She wove the reeds tightly together, row by row. She kissed her baby goodbye and trusted God to take care of him.
She set the basket among the tall reeds at the edge of the river. His big sister Miriam stood far off to see what would happen. The little basket bobbed gently on the water. Miriam hid quietly, watching her baby brother.
Pharaoh’s daughter, the princess, came down to the river to bathe. She saw the basket and opened it. The baby was crying! She felt sorry for him. She knew it was one of the Israelite babies. But she did not care about her father’s cruel rule. She wanted to keep him safe.
Miriam ran up and asked: Shall I find a mother to feed the baby for you? Yes, go! said the princess. And Miriam brought… the baby’s very own mother! The princess even paid the mother to take care of her own baby! What a happy surprise!
His mother cared for him until he was older. Then he went to live in the palace as the princess’s son. She named him Moses. God had saved him for a very special job! Moses means pulled out of the water. One day God would use Moses to lead all his people out of Egypt to freedom.
A mean Pharaoh wanted to hurt the Israelite baby boys. Moses’ mother hid him in a basket on the river. The princess found him, and God even let his own mother care for him. God protected baby Moses.

God opens the sea
Moses grew up, and God chose him to lead his people out of slavery. Moses went to Pharaoh and said: God says, let my people go! God had spoken to Moses from a bush that was on fire but did not burn up! Moses was nervous, but God promised to be with him.
Pharaoh said no, again and again. So God sent ten plagues: frogs everywhere, buzzing flies, hail, darkness and more. At last Pharaoh said: Go! With each plague, God showed that he is stronger than Pharaoh and all the false gods of Egypt.
All the Israelites left Egypt with their children, sheep and goats. God led them with a tall cloud by day and a pillar of fire by night. No more bricks, no more mean guards! They sang as they walked, free at last.
But Pharaoh changed his mind. He sent his army with horses and chariots after them! The people were trapped between the soldiers and the Red Sea. They were so afraid! Why did you bring us here? the people cried to Moses. It would have been better to stay in Egypt!
Moses said: Don’t be afraid! Stand still and watch how God will save you today. The Lord will fight for you. Then the tall cloud moved behind the people, between them and the soldiers. It was dark for the army, but it gave light to God’s people all night.
God told Moses to stretch out his staff over the sea. All night a strong wind blew. WHOOSH! The water split apart and made a dry path with a wall of water on each side! The water stood up tall like walls. The bottom of the sea became dry ground, without any mud!
The Israelites walked right through the middle of the sea on dry ground, all the way to the other side! Moms and dads carried their babies. Children held hands. Sheep and goats trotted along. They could see fish swimming in the walls of water!
When the army tried to follow, the water came back and covered them. God’s people were free! Miriam picked up her tambourine, and everyone danced and sang praises to God. They sang: The Lord is my strength and my song! He has saved us! They would never forget what God had done.
God used Moses to lead his people out of Egypt. When they were trapped at the Red Sea, God opened a path through the water, and they walked across on dry ground. Nothing is too hard for God!

The walls fall down
After Moses, God chose Joshua to lead his people into the land he had promised them. God said: Be strong and brave! I will be with you wherever you go. Joshua had been Moses’ helper for many years. Now it was his turn to lead.
The first city in their way was Jericho. It had huge, thick walls, and its gates were shut tight. No one could go in or out. The walls were so wide that people built houses on top of them. How could God’s people ever get inside?
God gave Joshua a very unusual plan: March around the city once a day for six days. Seven priests will carry trumpets made of ram’s horns in front of the ark of God. It was not a normal battle plan at all! But Joshua trusted that God’s way was the best way.
Joshua told the people: Don’t shout or say a word until I tell you! So every day they marched around the walls. Step, step, step. Only the trumpets made a sound. Day one, day two, day three… They walked around once and then went back to their camp to sleep.
The people of Jericho watched from the top of the walls. What are they doing? they wondered. Maybe they laughed. Maybe they were a little nervous. Nobody had ever seen anything like this before!
On the seventh day, they got up at dawn and marched around the city seven times! Once, twice, three times… all the way to seven! Their legs were tired, but they kept obeying.
The priests blew a long blast on the trumpets, and Joshua said: Shout! God has given you the city! Everyone shouted as loud as they could! Thousands of voices shouted together. The ground began to shake and rumble!
CRASH! The great walls of Jericho fell down flat! God’s people had obeyed his plan, and God gave them the victory. The people did not knock the walls down. God did! When we do things God’s way, we see how great he is.
God gave Joshua a strange plan: march around Jericho for seven days, blow the trumpets and shout. The people obeyed, and the big walls fell down! It is always good to obey God.

Ruth is a kind friend
There was no food in Bethlehem, so Naomi, her husband and their two sons moved to the land of Moab. Her sons married two women from Moab, Ruth and Orpah. Moab was a different country, with different customs. But the family loved each other, and for a while they were happy.
Then something very sad happened. Naomi’s husband died, and later both of her sons died too. Naomi was left alone with Ruth and Orpah. The three women cried together. Naomi felt that everything had gone wrong.
Naomi decided to go back home to Bethlehem. She told the young women: Go back to your mothers. Orpah kissed her goodbye. But Ruth held on to Naomi and would not leave. Naomi wanted the young women to be happy again in their own country. She did not want them to be poor and alone with her.
Ruth said: Don’t ask me to leave you! Where you go, I will go. Where you stay, I will stay. Your people will be my people, and your God will be my God. Ruth chose to love Naomi and to follow Naomi’s God, the one true God. That was a very brave and loving choice.
So Ruth and Naomi walked together all the way to Bethlehem. They arrived just when the barley harvest was beginning. When they arrived, the whole town came out. Is this really Naomi? they asked. Naomi said: I left full, but I came back empty.
They had no food, so Ruth went to the fields to pick up the grain the workers left behind. She worked hard from morning until evening. God’s law said farmers should leave some grain at the edges of their fields for poor people and strangers. Ruth was both!
The field belonged to a kind man named Boaz. He had heard how kind Ruth was to Naomi. He told his workers: Let her gather all she wants, and leave extra grain for her! Boaz even invited Ruth to eat lunch with his workers. Ruth went home with lots of grain, and Naomi was amazed: God has not forgotten us!
Later Boaz married Ruth, and God gave them a baby boy named Obed. Naomi held the baby close, so happy again! Obed became the grandfather of King David, and Jesus was born in their family. Naomi’s sadness turned into joy. God had taken care of them all along, through Ruth’s kindness and faithful love.
When Naomi lost her family, Ruth stayed with her and loved her faithfully. Ruth worked hard in Boaz’s field, and Boaz was kind to her. God blessed them with a baby, from the family of King David and Jesus.

God calls Samuel
Hannah wanted a baby very much. She prayed and prayed at God’s house. God answered her prayer, and she had a baby boy named Samuel. Hannah was so happy! She named him Samuel, which sounds like the words heard by God, because God had heard her prayer.
Hannah had promised to give her son to serve God. So when Samuel was still a little boy, he went to live at God’s house with Eli, the old priest. Every year Hannah visited Samuel and brought him a new little robe that she had made with love.
Samuel helped Eli every day. Eli was so old that he could hardly see. In those days, God did not speak to people very often. Samuel opened the doors in the morning, lit the lamps, and helped Eli with everything. He was learning to serve God.
One night, while the lamp of God was still burning, Samuel was lying down. Then he heard a voice: Samuel! It was very quiet. Everyone was asleep. Who could be calling him in the middle of the night?
Samuel ran to Eli: Here I am, you called me! Eli said: I didn’t call you. Go back and lie down. This happened two more times! Samuel did not know God’s voice yet. He thought it must be Eli. Each time, he ran as fast as he could.
The third time, Eli understood that God was calling the boy. He said: Go lie down. If he calls again, say: Speak, Lord, your servant is listening. Samuel went back to his bed with his heart beating fast. God wanted to talk to him, a little boy!
God came and called as before: Samuel! Samuel! And Samuel answered: Speak, for your servant is listening. Then God told Samuel what he was going to do. In the morning, Samuel was afraid to tell Eli. But Eli said: Tell me everything. And Samuel told the truth.
Samuel grew up, and God was with him. Everything Samuel said from God came true, and all the people knew that Samuel was God’s prophet. Later, God used Samuel to choose Israel’s kings, even young David. It all started with a little boy who said: I am listening.
Little Samuel lived in God’s house. One night God called his name. Samuel learned to answer: Speak, Lord, I am listening. Even children can hear God and obey him.

David and the giant
David was the youngest of eight brothers. He took care of his father’s sheep. When a lion or a bear tried to take a lamb, God helped David rescue it! Out in the fields David played his harp and sang songs to God. Many of his songs are in the Bible, in the book of Psalms.
The army of Israel was facing the army of the Philistines across a valley. The Philistines had a giant named Goliath. He was about three meters tall and wore heavy bronze armor! His spear was as big as a tree trunk! When he walked, the ground seemed to shake.
Every day for forty days, Goliath shouted: Send a man to fight me! King Saul and all his soldiers were terrified. Goliath laughed at them and made fun of God. Nobody wanted to fight such a giant!
David came to bring food to his brothers. He heard Goliath and said: Who is he to make fun of the army of the living God? I will fight him! His big brothers said: Go home, little brother! But David went to King Saul and said: Don’t lose heart! God will help me.
King Saul gave David his armor, but it was too big and heavy. David took it off. He took his shepherd’s staff and his sling, and picked five smooth stones from the stream. David did not need big armor. He trusted God and the simple things he knew how to use.
Goliath laughed at the boy. But David said: You come with a sword and a spear, but I come in the name of the Lord! The battle belongs to God! David was not proud. He wanted everyone to know that there is a God in Israel who saves his people.
David ran toward Goliath, put a stone in his sling and swung it round and round. Whoosh! The stone flew and hit Goliath on the forehead. David did not run away. He ran toward the giant! Just one stone, and God guided it straight to its target.
BOOM! The giant fell face down on the ground! The Philistines ran away, and Israel’s army cheered. God helped David win, and one day David became king. A shepherd boy with a sling had beaten a giant, because God was with him. With God, we don’t have to be afraid of big problems.
Everyone was afraid of the giant Goliath, except young David. David trusted God and used just a sling and a stone. God helped him defeat the giant! God can make us brave.

Jonah and the big fish
God spoke to a prophet named Jonah: Go to the great city of Nineveh. Tell the people that I have seen the bad things they do. Nineveh was the capital of a cruel enemy country. Jonah did not like those people at all.
But Jonah did not want to go. He ran away in the opposite direction and got on a ship sailing far away to Tarshish. Jonah thought he could hide from God. But nobody can hide from God, not even far out at sea.
God sent a great wind, and a terrible storm hit the ship. The sailors were scared and threw their cargo overboard. But Jonah was asleep down below! The captain woke Jonah up: How can you sleep? Get up and pray to your God! Maybe he will save us.
Jonah told them: This storm is my fault, because I ran away from God. Throw me into the sea, and it will become calm. So they did, and the sea grew still. The sailors did not want to throw him in. They rowed hard to get back to land, but the storm grew worse. When the sea became calm, the sailors worshiped God.
God sent a huge fish to swallow Jonah. GULP! Jonah was inside the fish for three days and three nights. It was dark and squishy inside the fish, and it smelled like seaweed. But Jonah was alive! God had saved him.
Inside the fish, Jonah prayed: In my trouble I called to you, Lord, and you heard me. I will do what I promised. God saves! Jonah was sorry for running away. He thanked God for not giving up on him.
God told the fish to spit Jonah out onto dry land. Then God spoke again: Go to Nineveh! This time, Jonah obeyed. Jonah walked all the way to the great city. It was so big it took three days to walk across it!
Jonah told the people of Nineveh God’s message. Everyone, from the king to the children, said sorry to God and stopped doing wrong. And God forgave them. God loves all people, even people we think are bad. He wants everyone to turn to him and be forgiven.
Jonah ran away from God, but God sent a storm and a big fish. Inside the fish Jonah prayed, and God gave him a second chance. Jonah obeyed, and the people of Nineveh turned back to God.
God gives second chances.
I called to the Lord, and he answered me.

Daniel and the lions
Daniel lived far from home in the land of Babylon. He loved God and was wise and honest, so King Darius made him one of his most important helpers. Daniel had been taken from his home when he was young. But even far away, he never stopped loving and obeying God.
The other leaders were jealous. They looked for something wrong that Daniel did, but they couldn’t find anything. So they made a sneaky plan. Daniel only did good things. The only thing they could find was that he prayed to God.
They told the king: Make a law that for thirty days, people can pray only to you, O king! Anyone who prays to anyone else will be thrown to the lions. The king signed it. The king liked being treated like a god, so he signed without thinking. Once signed, the law could never be changed.
Daniel heard about the law. But he went home, opened his windows toward Jerusalem, knelt down and prayed to God three times a day, just like always. Daniel was not afraid. He gave thanks to God, just as he had always done. He loved God more than his own safety.
The jealous men caught him and told the king. The king was very upset, because he liked Daniel. But the law could not be changed. All day long the king tried to find a way to save Daniel. But the jealous men reminded him: The law cannot be changed!
So Daniel was thrown into the den of lions, and a big stone was put over the opening. The king said: May your God, whom you serve, save you! The lions growled in the dark. But Daniel was calm. He knew God was with him, even in a den of lions.
That night the king could not sleep or eat. Early in the morning he hurried to the den and called: Daniel! Did your God save you? The king had worried all night. His voice was shaking as he called into the dark pit.
Daniel answered: Yes, O king! God sent his angel and shut the lions’ mouths. They didn’t hurt me at all! The happy king pulled Daniel out and told everyone to honor Daniel’s God. Not even a scratch was found on Daniel, because he trusted God. The king wrote to the whole world: Daniel’s God is the living God!
A law said no one could pray to God, but Daniel kept praying three times a day. He was thrown to the lions, but God sent an angel to shut their mouths. God kept Daniel safe!

Brave Queen Esther
Esther was a young Jewish girl who lived in Persia. Her parents had died, so her cousin Mordecai raised her like his own daughter. Esther was beautiful, but she was also kind and wise. Mordecai loved her and checked on her every day.
King Xerxes was looking for a new queen. Of all the young women, he liked Esther best, and he put a royal crown on her head. Esther became queen! Mordecai told Esther not to tell anyone yet that she was Jewish. Esther did as he said.
The king’s top official, Haman, was proud and cruel. Mordecai would not bow down to him, so Haman made an evil plan to destroy all the Jewish people. Haman tricked the king into signing a law against the Jews. Mordecai and all the Jewish people were very afraid and cried to God.
Mordecai sent a message to Esther: You must go to the king and ask him to save our people! Who knows? Maybe you became queen for such a time as this. Esther had a choice to make. Would she stay quiet and safe, or speak up for her people?
But anyone who went to the king without being called could be put to death! Esther said: Ask all our people to pray for me for three days. Then I will go, even if I die. For three days Esther did not eat. She prayed to God for courage and wisdom. She trusted God with her life.
Esther put on her royal robes and stood in the king’s courtyard. When the king saw her, he was pleased and held out his golden scepter. She was safe! Esther touched the tip of the scepter. The king said: What is your request, Queen Esther? I will give you up to half my kingdom!
Esther invited the king and Haman to a special dinner. There she told the king: Someone wants to destroy me and my people. The king asked: Who would do such a thing? Esther said: This wicked Haman! Haman’s face turned pale with fear. His evil plan had been discovered!
The king was very angry with Haman and stopped his evil plan. He honored Mordecai and protected the Jewish people. Everyone celebrated with joy, because God used brave Esther to save them! To this day, Jewish people remember Esther with a happy celebration called Purim. God had a plan, and Esther was brave enough to be part of it.
Esther became queen. When wicked Haman planned to destroy her people, Esther prayed and bravely went to the king. The king listened, and God’s people were saved.
